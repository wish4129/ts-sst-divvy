# Best Banking Stocks on Bursa Malaysia 2026: Maybank, Public Bank & HLB Compared

Banking stocks are the backbone of Bursa Malaysia. The sector makes up roughly a third of the FBM KLCI's weight, and when banks rally — like the July 2026 surge that saw Malayan Banking climb 1.6% and Hong Leong Bank 1.5% in a single session — the whole index moves with them.

For Malaysian investors, bank stocks offer a rare combination: meaningful dividend income, defensive earnings, and the liquidity to enter or exit positions easily. This guide compares the three largest listed banks on Bursa Malaysia — [Maybank](/stock/1155.KL/), [Public Bank](/stock/1295.KL/), and [Hong Leong Bank](/stock/5819.KL/) — across the metrics that actually matter.

## Why Banking Stocks Matter on Bursa Malaysia

Malaysia's banking sector is concentrated and well-capitalised. A handful of large banks dominate deposits, lending, and payment infrastructure, which gives them pricing power and remarkably stable earnings through economic cycles.

Three features make them core holdings for income investors:

- **Dividend consistency** — The major banks have paid uninterrupted dividends for decades, with payout ratios typically between 40-80%.
- **Macro tailwinds** — With Malaysia's Q2 2026 GDP growth at 5.8% (accelerating from 4.5% a year earlier), loan growth and asset quality are improving across the sector.
- **Liquidity** — Bank stocks are among the most actively traded on Bursa, making position sizing and exits straightforward.

## The Big Three Compared

### 1. Malayan Banking Berhad (Maybank) — [1155.KL](/stock/1155.KL/)

Maybank is Malaysia's largest bank by assets and market capitalisation, and the single heaviest stock in the FBM KLCI. Its regional footprint — Singapore, Indonesia, and Greater China — diversifies earnings beyond Malaysia's domestic cycle.

**What to like:** The largest and most liquid bank on the exchange; a fortress balance sheet; regional diversification that most peers can't match; a consistent dividend track record that makes it a favourite for yield-focused funds.

**What to watch:** Its size means growth is incremental rather than explosive, and its regional exposure ties part of its earnings to Singapore and Indonesian economic conditions.

**Best for:** Core portfolio holdings — investors who want the safest, most liquid way to own Malaysian banking.

### 2. Public Bank Berhad — [1295.KL](/stock/1295.KL/)

Public Bank is the retail banking champion, known for conservative lending practices and the highest efficiency in the Malaysian banking sector. Its founder-led culture of prudence has produced remarkably stable credit costs through multiple cycles.

**What to like:** Best-in-class asset quality — historically among the lowest impaired loan ratios in the country; strong retail deposit franchise; disciplined cost management; reliable dividend payer.

**What to watch:** Public Bank trades at a premium valuation to peers, reflecting its quality — which means less margin of safety if sentiment turns. Its growth is domestically focused.

**Best for:** Quality-over-growth investors who value stability and are willing to pay up for the sector's most conservatively run bank.

### 3. Hong Leong Bank Berhad — [5819.KL](/stock/5819.KL/)

Hong Leong Bank has quietly built a reputation as one of Malaysia's most efficient and profitable banks. With a strong digital banking push and a stake in Bank of Chengdu providing exposure to China's consumer credit market, it offers a different mix from the sector giants.

**What to like:** High return on equity relative to peers; growing digital banking franchise; the Bank of Chengdu associate adds an uncorrelated earnings stream.

**What to watch:** Smaller scale than Maybank and Public Bank; its China exposure cuts both ways — a tailwind when Chinese consumer credit performs, a headwind when it doesn't.

**Best for:** Investors comfortable with a slightly smaller, higher-growth banking play with a digital angle.

## Risks to Keep in Mind

Bank stocks are not risk-free, and 2026 has its own set of considerations:

- **Interest rate environment** — Malaysia's OPR sits at 2.75%. Rate decisions directly affect net interest margins, the core driver of bank profitability.
- **Asset quality** — Watch impaired loan ratios, especially for any bank with heavy exposure to segments under pressure (commercial property, SME lending).
- **Concentration** — Banking is a third of the KLCI. Owning multiple banks means you're making a sector bet, not just three individual bets.
- **Foreign flows** — Malaysian banks are a favourite of foreign institutional investors; their buying and selling amplifies both rallies and sell-offs.

## The Bottom Line

For Malaysian investors, bank stocks remain the highest-quality way to collect dividends while participating in the country's economic growth. [Maybank](/stock/1155.KL/) offers scale and safety, [Public Bank](/stock/1295.KL/) offers quality and consistency, and [Hong Leong Bank](/stock/5819.KL/) offers a growth and digital angle.

The smart approach is rarely "pick one" — it's deciding what role banking plays in your portfolio, then choosing the bank that fits that role. And with Malaysia's GDP accelerating in 2026, the sector's fundamentals are about as supportive as they've been in years.
