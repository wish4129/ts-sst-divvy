## Step 1: Understand What You're Buying

When you buy a stock on Bursa Malaysia, you're buying a tiny piece of a company. If the company does well, the stock price goes up and you can sell for a profit. Many Malaysian companies also pay **dividends** — a share of their profits distributed to shareholders, usually quarterly or semi-annually.

Bursa Malaysia has two main boards:

- **Main Market:** Large, established companies (Maybank, Tenaga Nasional, Public Bank, Petronas-linked). These are generally more stable.
- **ACE Market:** Smaller, growth-stage companies. Higher potential returns but higher risk.

There's also the **LEAP Market** for SMEs, but beginners should stick to the Main Market.

## Step 2: Open a CDS Account

A **Central Depository System (CDS)** account is where your stocks are held electronically. You need one before you can trade. Here's how:

### What You Need
- Malaysian IC (MyKad)
- A bank account (for funding)
- RM 10-20 for account opening fees (waived by many brokers)

## Step 3: Fund Your Trading Account

Once your CDS and trading accounts are approved:

1. Log into your broker's app
2. Find the "Deposit" or "Top Up" option
3. Transfer money from your bank account via FPX or bank transfer
4. Funds typically available within minutes (business hours) or same day

**Pro tip:** Start small. You can buy Malaysian stocks for as little as RM 100-200. You don't need thousands to begin.

## Step 4: Learn the Basics of Stock Research

Before buying your first stock, understand these four concepts:

### 1. P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)
How many years of earnings it takes to match the stock price. Lower P/E = potentially cheaper. The KLSE average is around 14-16x.

**Example:** If a stock is RM 2.00 and earned RM 0.10 per share last year, P/E = 20. You're paying 20 years of earnings.

### 2. Dividend Yield
The annual dividend divided by the stock price, expressed as a percentage. Malaysian stocks are famous for dividends.

**Example:** Maybank pays ~RM 0.60 dividend annually. At RM 10/share, that's a 6% yield — better than most fixed deposits.

### 3. Market Cap
The total value of all the company's shares. Large-cap (>RM 10 billion) = more stable. Small-cap (<RM 1 billion) = more volatile.

### 4. Volume
How many shares trade daily. Low volume = hard to buy/sell at your desired price. Stick to stocks trading at least 100,000 shares/day.

## Step 5: Pick Your First Stock

For your first investment, keep it simple. Here are sectors to consider:

### Banking (Safest Starter)
Malaysian banks are well-regulated and consistently profitable. They pay good dividends and are unlikely to go anywhere.
- Maybank (1155.KL) — Malaysia's largest bank, ~6% dividend yield
- Public Bank (1295.KL) — Known for conservative management, steady growth
- CIMB (1023.KL) — Regional exposure, higher growth potential

### REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
REITs own income-generating properties and must distribute 90% of profits as dividends. Great for regular income.
- Pavilion REIT (5212.KL) — Owns Pavilion KL, Elite Pavilion Mall
- Sunway REIT (5176.KL) — Diversified across retail, office, and hotels

### Consumer Staples
Companies selling things people buy regardless of the economy.
- Nestlé Malaysia (4707.KL) — Milo, Maggi, Kit Kat. Defensive stock.
- QL Resources (7084.KL) — Eggs, palm oil, fishery. Steady grower.

## Step 6: Place Your First Trade

Using Rakuten Trade as an example:

1. Search for the stock (e.g., "MAYBANK" or "1155")
2. Tap "Buy"
3. Enter the number of shares (100 shares = 1 lot minimum on Bursa, but Rakuten allows odd lots)
4. Choose order type:
   - **Market Order:** Buy at current price — fills immediately
   - **Limit Order:** Set your maximum price — fills only if market reaches that price
5. Review and confirm

Your first trade will feel nerve-wracking. That's normal. Everyone's first trade feels that way.

## Common Beginner Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Better Approach |
|---------|-----------------|
| Buying based on social media tips | Do your own research or use a screener |
| Panic-selling on red days | Market dips are normal. Wait it out. |
| Going all-in on one stock | Diversify across 5-10 stocks |
| Checking prices every hour | Investing is months/years, not hours |
| Ignoring fees | Brokerage is typically 0.05-0.10% per trade; factor it in |
