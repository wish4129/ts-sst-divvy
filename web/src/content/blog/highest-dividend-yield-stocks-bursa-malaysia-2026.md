Dividend investing is one of the most reliable wealth-building strategies on Bursa Malaysia. But chasing the highest yields blindly is a fast track to value traps — stocks whose dividends look great on paper but can't be sustained.

This list filters for **dividend sustainability**, focusing on companies with consistent payout histories, manageable payout ratios, and underlying businesses that generate real cash flow.

## REITs Dominate Bursa Dividend Investing

You'll notice REITs make up 6 of our top 10. This isn't a coincidence — Malaysian REITs are structurally designed for high yields:

1. **Mandatory distribution:** At least 90% of distributable income must be paid to unitholders
2. **Tax advantages:** REITs that distribute 90%+ are exempt from corporate tax on distributed income
3. **Asset-backed:** REITs own real physical property — a hard-asset floor under the investment
4. **Inflation hedge:** Property values and rents tend to rise with inflation over the long term

The trade-off? REITs are sensitive to interest rates. When rates rise, REIT yields must compete with risk-free alternatives like fixed deposits, and borrowing costs increase.

## Dividend Traps to Avoid

Before buying any dividend stock, check these warning signs:

| Red Flag | What It Means |
|----------|---------------|
| **Payout ratio > 100%** | The company is paying more in dividends than it earns — unsustainable. |
| **Falling revenue for 4+ quarters** | Declining business eventually means declining dividends. |
| **High debt with rising interest costs** | Interest payments crowd out dividends. |
| **One-off special dividends** | Don't annualize a special dividend — it won't repeat. |
| **Yield spiked because share price crashed** | A 10% yield might just mean the stock dropped 50%. Not a bargain — a warning. |
