Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) are one of the most popular vehicles for passive income on Bursa Malaysia. If you're looking for regular dividend payments without the headache of being a landlord, Malaysian REITs offer an accessible entry point into property investment.

This guide covers everything a beginner needs to know about REIT investing on Bursa Malaysia — from what REITs are and how they work, to the best REITs to buy in 2026, dividend schedules, tax treatment, and common pitfalls to avoid.

## What Is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-producing real estate. Think of it as a property mutual fund — you buy shares in a REIT, and the REIT uses pooled investor money to buy and manage properties like office buildings, shopping malls, hospitals, and hotels.

By law, Malaysian REITs must distribute **at least 90% of their taxable income** to shareholders as dividends. This is what makes them such powerful passive income vehicles.

### How REITs Are Different from Direct Property Investment

| Aspect | Direct Property | REIT |
|--------|----------------|------|
| Minimum investment | RM 300,000+ | RM 100-1,000 per share |
| Liquidity | Takes months to sell | Sell instantly on Bursa |
| Diversification | One property | Multiple properties across sectors |
| Management | You're the landlord | Professional management team |
| Entry barriers | Loan approvals, legal fees | Open a CDS + trading account |
| Monthly income | Rent (if tenanted) | Quarterly dividends |

## Types of REITs on Bursa Malaysia

Malaysia has **21 REITs** listed on Bursa Malaysia (as of mid-2026), spread across these main categories:

### Retail REITs — Shopping Malls & Retail Spaces
The largest category. Retail REITs own shopping centres, retail complexes, and commercial spaces anchored by long-term leases. Examples include Sunway REIT (malls like Sunway Pyramid), Pavilion REIT (Pavilion KL), and IGB REIT (Mid Valley Megamall).

**Yield range:** 4-7% | **Risk:** Moderate | **Best for:** Steady, predictable income from essential retail

### Office REITs — Commercial Office Buildings
Own office towers and commercial spaces in KL's business districts. Examples include KLCC REIT (Petronas Twin Towers, Menara 3 PETRONAS) and MRCB-Quill REIT.

**Yield range:** 5-7% | **Risk:** Moderate-Higher (office vacancy rates matter) | **Best for:** Diversification away from retail

### Industrial REITs — Warehouses, Factories, Logistics
The fastest-growing segment. Industrial REITs own warehouses, logistics centres, and manufacturing facilities. Driven by e-commerce growth and data centre demand.

**Yield range:** 5-6% | **Risk:** Lower (long-term leases, essential infrastructure) | **Best for:** Growth + income

### Hospitality REITs — Hotels & Resorts
Own hotel properties and resort assets. More cyclical — tied to tourism and business travel. Examples include Al-'Aqar Healthcare REIT (also has healthcare) and YTL Hospitality REIT.

**Yield range:** 5-8% | **Risk:** Higher (tourism-dependent) | **Best for:** Higher yield seekers comfortable with volatility

### Healthcare REITs — Hospitals & Medical Facilities
Own hospitals, medical office buildings, and healthcare facilities. Defensive sector with steady demand regardless of economic cycles.

**Yield range:** 4-6% | **Risk:** Lower | **Best for:** Defensive income allocation

## How to Start Investing in REITs

### Step 1: Open a CDS Account
A Central Depository System (CDS) account is required to hold shares. Open one through any Bursa Malaysia participating bank or stockbroking firm (CIMB, Maybank, Rakuten Trade, etc.).

### Step 2: Open a Trading Account
Choose a broker — Rakuten Trade (low fees, beginner-friendly), CIMB Clicks, Maybank Invest, or traditional brokers like Kenanga or KAF.

### Step 3: Fund Your Account
Deposit funds via online banking. Most brokers accept instant fund transfers.

### Step 4: Research Your REITs
Use Divvy ([screener](/screener)) to compare REITs by dividend yield, payout ratio, NAV, gearing ratio, and management expense ratio. Don't just pick the highest yield — check whether the dividend is sustainable.

### Step 5: Buy
Place your order through your broker's trading platform. Minimum purchase is typically 100 units (1 board lot). At RM 1.50 per share, that's RM 150.

### Step 6: Set Up Dividend Collection
Dividends are paid directly into your CDS-linked bank account. Most Malaysian REITs pay dividends **quarterly** (every 3 months).

## Tax Treatment of REIT Dividends in Malaysia

Understanding the tax implications is crucial for accurate return calculations:

- **Malaysian-resident individual investors:** REIT dividends are typically paid **tax-free** — the REIT has already paid 24% corporate tax on the income before distributing it to you. You receive the dividend net of tax with no further withholding or personal taxation.
- **Non-resident investors:** Withholding tax of 10% applies to REIT dividends.
- **Corporate investors:** REIT dividends are taxed at the corporate tax rate (24%).

## REIT Risks to Know

### Interest Rate Sensitivity
REITs are sensitive to interest rates. When rates rise, REIT prices typically fall (because higher bond yields make REIT dividends less attractive by comparison). 2025-2026 has seen some normalisation of interest rates, so factor this into your entry timing.

### Property Market Cycles
A downturn in commercial property values affects NAV. However, Malaysian REITs have historically been resilient — the rental income stream is what drives dividends, not NAV fluctuations.

### Concentration Risk
Some REITs are heavily exposed to one property or one tenant. Pavilion REIT is highly dependent on Pavilion KL's performance. Diversify across REITs with different property types and locations.

### Gearing (Debt) Risk
Malaysian REITs are limited to 50% gearing (debt-to-total-asset ratio). The lower the gearing, the safer the dividend. Avoid REITs with gearing above 45% unless you understand their refinancing plan.

## Common REIT Investing Mistakes

1. **Chasing the highest yield** — A 9% yield on a REIT with 95% payout ratio and 48% gearing is not a bargain. It's a red flag.
2. **Ignoring the gearing ratio** — REITs with high debt are vulnerable to interest rate hikes. Stay below 45% gearing.
3. **Not diversifying** — Don't put everything in retail REITs just because malls feel safe. Mix in industrial, office, and healthcare.
4. **Forgetting transaction costs** — Brokerage fees, clearing fees, and stamp duty eat into small portfolios. Keep trades to RM 500+ to keep fees under 1%.
5. **Panic selling on rate hikes** — REIT prices dip on rate hike news, but dividends keep flowing. Hold through the cycle.

## FAQs

### What's the minimum to start REIT investing in Malaysia?
With REIT share prices ranging from RM 0.50 to RM 7.00, you can start with as little as RM 100-200 per REIT. A diversified portfolio of 5 REITs is achievable with RM 1,000-2,000.

### Are REIT dividends guaranteed?
No. While REITs are legally required to distribute 90% of taxable income, if the REIT's properties generate less income (e.g., vacancy rises), dividends will fall. That said, Malaysian REITs have strong track records — many haven't cut dividends in a decade.

### How are REIT dividends taxed?
For Malaysian resident individual investors, REIT dividends are received tax-free. The REIT has already paid corporate tax on the income.

### Can I reinvest REIT dividends?
Most REITs don't offer DRP (Dividend Reinvestment Plans). You'll receive cash dividends and can manually buy more shares on Bursa through your trading account.

### Are REITs better than fixed deposits?
For long-term income, yes. REITs yield 4-7% versus FD rates of 2.5-3%. But REIT prices fluctuate — they're not capital-guaranteed like FDs.
